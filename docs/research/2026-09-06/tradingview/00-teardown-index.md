# TradingView teardown — index

Written 2026-09-06 as the front page of the teardown in this folder: thirteen surface documents (01–13) and, added the same day after the critic's pass, five fillers (14–18) for the gaps it found. The teardown is the reference material for OnStock's desktop ("on a PC it should be like TradingView"). The adoption decisions live in `../onstock-desktop-spec.md` (revised for 14–18 the same day); this page only says what each document covers, what matters most in it, and what the whole set says about why TradingView feels the way it does.

**One errata rule before anything else.** Document 15 read the pricing table from the page's raw HTML and found that 01, 03, 04, 05, 07, 10 and 13 had each misread at least one plan cell (08 has all 109 right; 02, 06 and 12 were right on the cells they quoted), because the table draws ✓ / ✗ / ∞ as icons with no text and a text fetcher sees blanks (a "✗ / 2 / 4 / 6 / ∞" row reads as "2 / 4 / 6" shifted one plan left). **For any plan number, cite 08 and 15; not 01, 05, 07, 10 or 13.** 15 §4 lists all 47 corrections; the two that reached the spec (the "everything works on Basic" claim, item 16 below, and the "≈17 % annual saving" wording) are fixed in both places. 18 §4's read of "Financials" as Plus+ is one more such misread — 15 §3.10 has it ✓ on every plan.

Two companion documents from the day before are cited throughout the spec and are **not on this branch**: `docs/research/2026-09-05/desktop-audit.md` and `docs/research/2026-09-05/tradingview-charts-license.md` exist only on the `research/2026-09-05` branch (commit `ca75c7b`). Read them with `git show research/2026-09-05:docs/research/2026-09-05/<file>` until that branch is merged.

Plan names as TradingView prints them today: **Basic** (free), **Essential** ($12.95/mo annual, $14.95 monthly), **Plus** ($29.95 / $34.95), **Premium** ($59.95 / $69.95), **Ultimate** ($199.95 / $239.95); a hidden **Expert** SKU (`pro_expert`, $99.95 / $119.95) sits between Premium and Ultimate and has no column on the page. Prices and gates were read from the pricing page on 2026-09-06 (raw HTML, 15 §2) and can move; the page's own schema data says `priceValidUntil: 2026-10-06`.

## The things that make TradingView feel like TradingView

Pulled across all eighteen documents: the details users mention when they say another chart "doesn't feel right". Each is annotated with where OnStock stands today (from the code read on 2026-09-06) so the spec's Phase 1 list is traceable.

1. **Auto-scale that you can see.** The price scale fits the data by default, dragging the axis turns Auto off, and an **Auto** button under the scale (plus "Reset price scale" / Alt+R) turns it back on. OnStock: Lightweight Charts autoscale is on, but there is no Auto chip and `fitContent()` runs on every data refresh, which throws away the user's zoom every 30 seconds in Candles mode.
2. **The last-price label and line.** A coloured tag on the scale at the current price with a dotted line across the chart; a previous-close line available but off by default. OnStock: the candle series keeps the library defaults (label and line on) but nothing is exposed; the Gap chart has neither.
3. **Countdown to bar close** inside the last-price tag — and honestly hidden when the data is delayed by more than the interval. OnStock: none; the rule maps cleanly (token candles yes, delayed Yahoo stock candles no).
4. **Type-to-act.** Any letter opens symbol search with that letter already typed; any digit opens the interval prompt (bare number = minutes, D/W/M suffixes); Ctrl+K is quick search; `/` is indicators; Ctrl+/ lists every shortcut. OnStock: no keyboard handling on the chart at all.
5. **Crosshair that snaps and syncs.** Magnet mode snaps the crosshair to OHLC; with several charts, Crosshair sync moves every chart's readout together; "Lock vertical cursor line by time" freezes it. OnStock: `CrosshairMode.Normal`, and the Gap chart's scrub does not reach the gap-history pane under it.
6. **The status line (legend).** Symbol, interval, OHLC, change, volume and each indicator's values in the top-left of the pane, with hover buttons per row. OnStock: a caption row above the chart with O/H/L/C and a time.
7. **The right toolbar model.** A 45-px strip of icons; each opens a panel beside the chart at 300 px (floor 200, collapses below it, grows until the chart is 296); the Watchlist panel holds a Details section beneath it behind a draggable divider (50/50 by default); those panels are account-wide and survive layout changes; the lower icons open full products as overlays with **Move overlay to split-view** (17 §2, 18 §0). OnStock: a permanent 448 px watchlist pane on the left of the docked asset screen.
8. **Single click vs double click** in the watchlist, and ↓/↑ stepping through it. OnStock: single click switches the asset; no keys.
9. **Layouts you can bookmark.** Named, autosaved, one URL each, remembering symbol + interval + indicators, with Make a copy and read-only sharing. OnStock: no layouts, no URL state.
10. **Templates and defaults everywhere.** Indicator templates (with Remember Symbol), chart templates (a colour set), drawing templates, per-indicator "Save as default". OnStock: none; three fixed indicator toggles.
11. **Right-click does the obvious thing.** "Add alert on AAPL at 317.66…", "Copy price", "Lock vertical cursor line by time", "Object tree", "Settings…". OnStock: no context menu.
12. **Alert marks that live on the chart.** Draggable, blinking on fire, optional line across the chart, "Only active alerts" filter. OnStock: alerts are a rule per asset and a feed on the News tab.
13. **Measure with Shift+click** (Δ price, %, bars, elapsed time) and **click-and-hold for the data window**. OnStock: neither; the Gap chart's press-and-drag scrub is the closest thing.
14. **Go to date** (Alt+G) with a red marker at the first available bar when history runs out. OnStock: none; would also disclose GeckoTerminal's shallow history honestly.
15. **Keep your place when switching interval** ("Save chart left edge position when changing interval"). OnStock: refit on every change.
16. **The chart is the last thing Basic loses — but it does lose some of it.** An earlier version of this item said "everything works on Basic"; the HTML read in 15 §3.17 shows 46 Basic cells are crosses, including intervals (custom, second, tick), four chart types, Volume Profile, auto patterns, intraday replay and bar depth, alongside the quantity gates (charts per tab, indicators, layouts, alerts). What is true: every *standard* chart type, the indicator library, all drawings, Compare, extended hours, daily replay, Pine and the desktop app are free, and the headline gates are counts, delivery and depth. OnStock's posture — never gate inside the chart at all — is stricter than TradingView's and rests on the Advanced Charts licence (spec §5.1), not on TradingView's example.
17. **Settings follow the chart slot, not the symbol.** Clicking down a watchlist keeps interval, chart type and indicators. OnStock: `AssetDetail` remounts on every switch (`key={openAssetId}`), so interval, mode and indicator toggles reset.
18. **A restrained canvas.** 6 %-alpha grid, no axis rule, dashed grey crosshair, off-chroma up/down, 12 px scale text, session shading in a third hue. OnStock's design system already agrees on all but the session shading and the text-size step.
19. **Session honesty.** A market-status button in the legend that reads `Market closed · One update every 5 seconds · NASDAQ by Cboe One` (session · cadence · venue **by** source), a yellow **D** on delayed symbols with "Quotes are delayed by 15 min" on hover, a pop-up with the session ruler in the exchange's time zone, extended-hours tints, "Data is delayed" with an explanation (16 §1). OnStock has better copy for this than TradingView (the four-state session strip, per-leg ages) but only on the phone Home screen and under the chart, never in the legend.
20. **The connection budget.** Every tab is a connection; layouts are cheaper than tabs; when the cap is hit a pop-up says what happened and offers one **Restore connection** button. OnStock's equivalent is upstream API quota, and the desktop shell must not spend more than the phone does — the pop-up's shape is worth copying for a GeckoTerminal 429, the counting is not.
21. **The tab title is the quote.** `AAPL 319.97 ▼ −2.51%` — ticker, price, a real triangle, a real minus sign, no product name; ticker-only before the first quote; the layout name appended when one is open (17 §4). OnStock: a static title. The spec adopts the grammar and adds the one thing TradingView cannot write there: the gap.
22. **A frame that is measured, not breakpointed.** Toolbars 38 px, gutters 4 px, rails 52 / 45 px, the panel 300 px, rows 31 px, the legend 4 px in from the corner — and a toolbar that decides its own mode by measuring itself against hidden clones, dropping labels before buttons and buttons before the chart (17 §1, §3). OnStock: 48 / 64 / 40 / 320 were guesses; the spec's §1 now carries the measured numbers and the order of sacrifice.
23. **One grammar for the details.** Under the watchlist, a fixed header (name · exchange · tags · price · **D**/**R** · "Last update at …") then titled blocks in a fixed order, each with one "More …" button, hideable from a synced checklist; every number visible to a guest, only the account actions gated by a sign-in dialog (18 §1). OnStock: the gap panel is block 0; the long tail is in tabs under the chart by the owner's choice (spec §7 Q15).
